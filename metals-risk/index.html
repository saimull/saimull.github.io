How this works

Both metals scores use RSI over 14 available observations (20%), extension relative to the mean of available prices in the past 200 calendar days (30%), and elapsed-time-adjusted sampled volatility over 30 days (25%).

Gold uses its own decline from the highest sampled close in the past 60 calendar days for the remaining 25%. This factor rises from 0 at the peak to 1 at a 20% decline and captures recent losses that can persist even after RSI and price extension fall.

Silver uses the inverse gold/silver ratio percentile over three years for the remaining 25%. It pairs each silver price with the latest available gold price, up to seven days old. Older history is sampled, so the scores can differ with sampling density.

The Bitcoin score uses Coinbase BTC/USD daily closing prices.

  • Downside volatility (40%). Measures the size of down days over the past 30 daily returns. The annualized measure scores zero at 20% or below and reaches 1 at 85%.
  • 90-day drawdown (35%). Measures the decline from Bitcoin’s highest daily close in the past 90 days. The factor reaches 1 at a 45% drawdown.
  • 200-day trend weakness (25%). Measures how far Bitcoin trades below its 200-day average. The factor starts adding points below that average and reaches 1 at 35% below it.

This is an exploratory composite, not a calibrated probability of loss. The weighting and thresholds have not been statistically validated. Do not use it alone to time trades or invest.
