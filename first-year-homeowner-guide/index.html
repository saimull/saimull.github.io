FREE HOMEOWNER GUIDE

The First-Year Homeowner Survival Guide

What to buy, what to delay, and what to avoid after getting the keys.

You bought the house. You don't need to buy the entire lifestyle overnight.
The mortgage is only one part of the budget. Protect your savings while making the home your own.
01 / FIRST 30 DAYS

Make it safe and functional

Rekey locks or confirm access, test smoke and carbon monoxide alarms, locate water shutoff and electrical panel, set up utilities and internet, and address leaks or safety issues. Buy only the furniture, cleaning supplies, basic tools, and privacy coverings you actually need. Not every home needs every item.

02 / FIRST 90 DAYS

Live there before you upgrade

A larger TV, matching bedroom sets, guest-room furniture, decorative lighting, smart-home gadgets, and garage epoxy can usually wait. Learn how you use each room first. Exception: painting or flooring may be less expensive before moving furniture in.

03 / FIRST YEAR

Watch the lifestyle creep

A bigger living room doesn't require a bigger TV. An empty bedroom doesn't require a bedroom set. A backyard doesn't require a pergola, grill, and patio makeover. A two-car garage doesn't require a second car. Ask whether the purchase solves a real problem or just fills space.

04 / ONGOING

Budget for ownership, not just the mortgage

Property taxes, insurance, HOA dues, utilities, maintenance, lawn care, pest control, appliance replacement, and unexpected repairs can affect cash flow. These aren't automatically lifestyle inflation. They are part of owning a home, and some will rise over time.

05 / MODEL HOME ILLUSION

Don't confuse the display with the deal

Model homes are designed to sell a feeling. Furniture, window coverings, landscaping, premium finishes, and appliances on display may be upgrades or not included. Check the contract and included-features sheet before assuming you'll get what you saw.

06 / SEVEN RULES

Protect your cash after closing

1. Keep emergency savings available.
2. Fix safety and water issues before cosmetic ones.
3. Don't furnish rooms you don't use.
4. Compare the total price, not just monthly furniture payments.
5. Avoid buying upgrades to impress neighbors.
6. Give nonurgent purchases a waiting period.
7. Choose durable, useful items when the budget allows.

The question to ask before every purchase

Do I need this now, will it improve daily life, and can I pay for it without weakening my emergency fund?

Spending isn't inherently bad. Quality furniture, essential maintenance, and thoughtful improvements can be worthwhile. The problem is spending automatically because the house is bigger or because someone else's home looks better.

Educational information only, not individualized financial advice. Timing and priorities depend on your home's condition, climate, budget, and household needs.
