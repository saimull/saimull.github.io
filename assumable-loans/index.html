01 / What you actually inherit

The loan transfers. The clock usually doesn't restart.

With a typical approved FHA or VA assumption, you take over the outstanding balance, existing rate, and remaining repayment schedule. You buy the house at today's agreed price—not what the seller originally paid.

“Assumable” does not mean automatic

You still need the required approval. The servicer generally reviews your credit, income, debts, and funds. The seller must agree to the transaction.

Start with the existing servicer's assumption department. A listing description is a lead—not proof of approval.

03 / Which loans qualify?

Check the loan type before chasing the rate.

Loan typeWhat a buyer should know
FHAGenerally assumable, subject to applicable approval and credit requirements. Confirm continuing mortgage insurance and its cancellation rules for that specific loan.
VAQualified non-veterans can also assume VA loans. Approval and the seller's release from liability matter. The seller's entitlement may stay tied up unless an eligible veteran substitutes entitlement.
USDAAssumptions have program-specific eligibility and approval rules. Do not assume the existing rate and terms will carry over unchanged.
ConventionalMost are not assumable in an ordinary home sale. Some exceptions exist; have the servicer verify the actual loan documents.

Being a first-time buyer does not itself create an exemption from assumption requirements.

04 / Look beyond principal and interest

A fixed rate is not a fixed housing budget.

The seller's escrow payment is not your insurance quote or a guarantee of your future tax bill. Build your budget from your own estimates.

  • First mortgage + any second-loan payment
  • Property taxes and homeowner's insurance
  • Continuing mortgage insurance, if applicable
  • HOA dues, maintenance, and repair reserves
  • Closing, assumption, title, and other applicable fees
Decision rule

Don't overpay for the privilege of a low rate.

Compare the asking price with similar homes. Then compare cash required, total monthly costs, and costs over the years you expect to own it.

A large cash gap can drain your emergency fund. An expensive second loan can shrink the savings. A good mortgage cannot fix a house that doesn't fit your budget.

05 / Common traps

Questions worth asking early.

Can I just take over the seller's payments?

A private agreement to make payments while the loan stays in the seller's name is not the same as an approved assumption. A formal assumption makes you an accepted borrower under the lender's agreement. Have the servicer and closing professional document the transfer and seller's release from liability.

Will a second mortgage cover the equity gap?

Possibly, but do not build an offer around unapproved financing. Ask whether secondary financing is permitted, which lenders will offer it, and how the combined payments affect qualification. Get its rate, term, fees, and any balloon payment in writing. The calculator uses fully amortizing fixed-rate loans without balloons.

Why might a VA seller prefer another veteran?

Release from liability and restoration of VA entitlement are different issues. A qualified veteran buyer may substitute entitlement. With a non-veteran buyer, the seller's entitlement generally remains tied to the assumed loan, which can limit their next VA purchase.

How long does an assumption take?

Timing varies by servicer and file. Get a written estimate and a document checklist before setting your closing date. Have the contract address assumption approval, deadlines, possible extensions, and return of earnest money if the required financing is not approved.

Do I still need an inspection and title review?

Yes—treat the property's condition and ownership history as separate from its financing. Arrange an appropriate inspection and title review. Ask about other liens, deferred balances, loan modifications, and amounts that must be paid at closing.
